Security Incident Update: SKALE IMA Bridge

By Mara Velez Sep 4, 2026 2 min read

SKALE’s IMA Bridge failed at the validator layer that was supposed to secure it. On August 27 at about 21:00 UTC, infrastructure providers running SKALE validator nodes were compromised, allowing an attacker to drain ERC-20 assets from the Ethereum-connected bridge serving the Europa Liquidity Hub. SKALE paused the bridge, but as of September 4 had disclosed neither the value stolen nor the affected wallets—leaving users without a defensible measure of the shortfall. The separate SKALE on Base deployment was not affected, according to the network’s incident update.

What did the attacker compromise?

The disclosed entry point was validator infrastructure, but SKALE has not said which providers were breached, how many nodes were affected or whether the attacker extracted signing shares, hijacked running services or used another route to authorize withdrawals.

That omission cuts directly into IMA’s security model. SKALE Labs is the core technical contributor, while independent SKALE validators operate the bridge’s signing infrastructure. An off-chain IMA Agent carries messages between paired contracts on Ethereum and SKALE chains; validator nodes attest to those messages with BLS threshold signatures before receiving contracts act on them. Each SKALE chain is normally supported by 16 randomly selected nodes, and SKALE’s consensus documentation sets the supermajority threshold at 11 signatures for a 16-node committee.

SKALE has not confirmed that 11 signing shares were compromised or explained how a malicious message passed verification. Until it publishes that path, calling the incident “contained” describes geography, not the integrity of the authorization system.

Which assets and chains were exposed?

The confirmed exposure was limited to ERC-20 assets associated with Europa and the IMA deployment connected to Ethereum mainnet. SKALE said native SKL on Ethereum and staked SKL remained secure, while SKALE on Base continued operating because it uses separate infrastructure and architecture.

IMA uses a lock-and-mint model: ERC-20 tokens deposited into Ethereum contracts back corresponding tokens minted on a SKALE chain. On the return path, tokens are burned on SKALE and the Ethereum deposit box releases the backing asset. SKALE’s bridge documentation makes that dependency explicit. Compromise the message-authorizing infrastructure and the locked collateral becomes the target.

The exposed dollar value remains undisclosed. SKALE has promised a report listing affected wallets and is tracing assets, seeking freezes and working with authorities, but it has not published a transaction-level accounting, recovery total or reopening date.

What must SKALE prove before reopening?

SKALE must identify the breached operators, quantify the stolen tokens and show why the same compromise cannot again satisfy—or bypass—the bridge’s signing threshold. IMA gives users transfers advertised at under 30 seconds and gives a small rotating committee control over cross-chain releases. Users receive speed; validators receive that control; depositors inherit the correlated infrastructure risk. Reopening without a verified exploit route, rotated credentials and reconciled backing would restore convenience before restoring security.
