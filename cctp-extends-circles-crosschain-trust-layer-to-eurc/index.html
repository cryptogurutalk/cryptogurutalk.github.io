CCTP Extends Circle’s Crosschain Trust Layer to EURC

Circle switched on native EURC transfers through Cross-Chain Transfer Protocol between Ethereum and Base on September 2, replacing wrapped-token and…

By Mara Velez Sep 4, 2026 2 min read

Circle switched on native EURC transfers through Cross-Chain Transfer Protocol between Ethereum and Base on September 2, replacing wrapped-token and liquidity-pool risk with a cleaner but explicitly centralized dependency on Circle’s own signing infrastructure. The launch extends CCTP beyond USDC for the first time and puts Circle Technology Services LLC, the protocol’s operator, in control of the message path for a euro stablecoin with €398 million in circulation as of September 3.

How does EURC move between Ethereum and Base?

EURC is burned on the source chain and an equal amount is minted on the destination chain, so no third-party custodian locks coins in a bridge vault and no wrapped EURC claim is created. A user calls CCTP’s TokenMessengerV2 contract, which delegates the burn to TokenMinterV2 and emits a crosschain message containing the amount, recipient and destination domain.

Circle’s offchain Attestation Service, called Iris, observes the source-chain transaction after the required confirmation level and signs that message. A relayer or user then submits the message and attestation to MessageTransmitterV2 on the destination chain, where the signature is checked before native EURC is minted. A nonce prevents the same message from being used twice. Circle’s launch announcement names Ethereum and Base as the first EURC route; EURC on Avalanche, Solana and Stellar is not yet included in this CCTP expansion.

Who controls the minting trust?

Circle does. CCTP does not verify Ethereum state independently on Base, or Base state independently on Ethereum; the destination contract accepts the account of events signed by Circle-controlled attesters. L2BEAT’s current CCTP assessment reports a two-of-two signer threshold for V2, meaning both configured attesters must authorize a message.

The contracts also preserve administrative control: an attester manager can change signers and the threshold, the owner can replace that manager, and V2 proxy administrators can upgrade contract implementations without an onchain delay. That is the decisive trade-off. Developers receive one integration, native liquidity and fewer bridge components; Circle retains the authority, while EURC holders inherit signer compromise, censorship, pausing and upgrade risk.

How much value is actually exposed?

CCTP does not park the entire €398 million EURC supply in a honeypot-style bridge pool. The immediate crosschain exposure is the value of transfers awaiting a valid destination mint, while the broader supply remains subject to Circle’s issuance, reserve and contract controls. Circle Internet Financial Europe SAS issues EURC under a full-reserve model and promises redemption at one euro.

The design is materially better than trusting an unrelated bridge operator with pooled collateral, but it is not trustless interoperability. EURC gains a canonical Ethereum–Base lane precisely because Circle controls both the stablecoin and the signatures that authorize its crosschain minting.
